Statistics

CORAL BLEACHING CONTINUES TO UNFOLD AS OCEAN TEMPURATURE REACH RECORD HIGH

The ocean absorbs around 90% of excess heat trapped by greenhouse gases, and ocean heat content reached a record high in 2024. This warming threatens marine ecosystems, particularly coral reefs, which support about a quarter of marine life and approximately one billion people worldwide. Between January 2023 and May 2025, bleaching-level heat stress affected 84% of the world’s coral reefs across 83 countries and territories. Furthermore, 14% of global coral cover was lost between 2009 and 2018, while 44% of coral species now face extinction. If global warming reaches 1.5°C, 70–90% of warm-water coral reefs could disappear by 2050, threatening marine biodiversity, food security and coastal communities.

OCEAN ACIDIFICATION IS A RISING GLOBAL THREAT

The ocean absorbs approximately 25% of human-caused CO₂ emissions annually, helping regulate the climate but increasing ocean acidification. According to the WMO, average ocean pH declined from 8.1 in 1985 to 8.04 in 2023, threatening coral reefs, marine biodiversity, coastal protection and food security. Some regions, including parts of the Indian and Pacific Oceans, are acidifying faster than the global average. Although monitoring stations increased from 178 in 2021 to 765 in 2025, significant data gaps remain, highlighting the need for improved monitoring to understand ocean acidification and protect marine ecosystems.

IMPLEMENTATION OF INTEGRATED OCEAN MANAGEMENT

Sustainable ocean management is essential as marine ecosystems face threats from climate change, pollution and habitat loss. Approaches such as marine spatial planning (MSP) and integrated coastal zone management (ICZM) help protect marine habitats, reduce conflicts and promote sustainable ocean use. In 2023, 126 countries and territories were involved in MSP initiatives, a 20% increase from 2022, but only 45 had formally approved marine spatial plans. Challenges such as limited funding, data gaps and weak coordination remain, highlighting the need for stronger policies and international cooperation to protect marine ecosystems and the communities that depend on them.

SUSTAINABILITY CHALLENGES PERSIST, MANAGEMENT DRIVES PROGRESS

In 2021, 64.5% of marine fish stocks were fished at biologically sustainable levels, while 35.5% were overfished. Although this represents a slight improvement from the previous estimate of 62.3%, the proportion of overfished stocks continues to increase by approximately 1% annually, highlighting the urgent need for more effective fisheries management. Regional differences are significant, with the Northeast and Southwest Pacific recording sustainable fishing rates of 92.7% and 85.5%, respectively, while the Mediterranean Sea, Black Sea and Southeastern Pacific continue to face major sustainability challenges.

Additionally, 77.2% of global fish landings came from sustainably fished stocks, suggesting that fisheries producing larger catches are often better managed. Tuna fisheries also demonstrate how effective management practices and international cooperation can support sustainable fishing. These findings highlight the importance of stronger fishing regulations, responsible harvesting and improved cooperation between countries to reduce overfishing, protect marine biodiversity and preserve fish populations for future generations.
